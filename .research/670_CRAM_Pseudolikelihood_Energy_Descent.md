## TL;DR

CRAM resurrects the Hopfield–Tank (1985) pairwise energy as a mean-field free-energy form with LEARNED weights — a tabular MRF: per-cell input costs B_i, per-cell-pair cost tables K_ij — trains it with pseudolikelihood, a LOCAL objective evaluated at the labeled solution with NO inference in the loop and NO BPTT, and reasons by discrete descent: clamp the givens, run several single-cell-move chains, stop at a fitted level. The honest headline: on the four benchmarks (Sudoku-Extreme, Maze-Unique, N-Queens, Graph-Coloring) a hand-set version with ≤3 unique weight values solves 100% of every test set, so benchmark accuracy certifies "learned a few pairwise rules," not scalable reasoning — the paper says so itself. What survives for this stack: (1) local objectives annihilate BPTT on exactly-representable tasks (PL: 99% dev in 48 ms; full BPTT FAILS at 48.8%/5 h); (2) a fitted-LEVEL stop threshold (highest training-solution energy + tolerance) never stops at a wrong board while all five looped halting rules do (0.05–3.54%) — a level-vs-dynamics signal the workspace's halt census does not carry; (3) descent-search POLICY deltas — K chains + lowest-score pick, abstain-on-budget — that extend the shipped `katgpt-assign` local-search substrate (Research 607/Plan 620; the driver itself is occupied — the first sweep missed it under "local search" vocabulary); (4) an unpublished fusion class the owner asked for: rating-energized landscapes (Elo/Beta input costs + co-outcome pair costs) descended at test time. Three consumer filings; training rows onto Issue 682.

**Distilled for katgpt-rs (modelless, inference-time):** the runtime residue is one ABSENT mechanism with a clean modelless extraction — a fitted-level stop calibrated on solved-case scores (the quantile/conformal machinery the stack already owns) — plus descent-policy deltas (K chains, abstain-on-budget) on the shipped `katgpt-assign` substrate. The self-adaptive residue: Elo/Beta updates ARE the no-BPTT shaping rule for decision landscapes — landscape weights come from outcome statistics the stack already records.

## Claim check (the owner's question: "1,000× faster training and 280× less compute — if true")

**TRUE, scoped, and self-deflating in the right way.**

- Training ≥1,000× vs seven retrained looped baselines (authors' code + recipes, one H200; CRAM = warm median-of-7, baselines = cold — disclosed in-paper). The transferable fact is not the 1,000× but the objective ranking: PL 48 ms → 99% dev; min probability flow 0.21 s; one-step BPTT 4.4 s; CD 33 s; FULL BPTT 5 h → 48.8% (fails). Local objectives win because the task class is exactly representable by the energy.
- Inference: ≥282× fewer FLOPs than EBR (the cheapest looped cousin) at EBR's top accuracy; up to 128,000× vs EqR. CRAM "never returns a wrong answer, only misses" (51 budget-exhaustions in 1,238,358 searches).
- The deflation: hand-set energies solve every test puzzle on every benchmark — Sudoku's hand-set row is ONE unique value (Table 2), Maze's three — and the LEARNED weights independently land on exactly the 810 row/col/box pairs (Fig. 2b). Corollary for the stack: the sotaku lane's Sudoku numbers (99.12% 25K-Sudoku, Research 035's table) need a hand-set/energy-baseline control row before advancing as reasoning progress (filed on Issue 682).
- Concurrent-work note: EBR (Research 663, five days earlier) already holds "replace recurrence with energy descent" at neural scale; CRAM's edge is the LOCAL objective (no BPTT), the level-stop, and the tiny tabular form — not the energy idea per se.

## 1. Paper Core Findings

1. Energy (Eq. 1): E_θ(p) = β⁻¹Σᵢ pᵢᵀlog pᵢ + ½Σ_{i≠j} pᵢᵀK_ij pⱼ + Σᵢ Bᵢᵀpᵢ — mean-field free energy (Peterson & Soderberg 1989 lineage); beliefs pᵢ = softmax(βvᵢ); W_ij = (K_ij+K_jiᵀ)/2 pops from the gradient; deterministic descent guarantees dE/dt ≤ 0 (App. E). Focused Gibbs (updates prioritized by local energy) escapes local minima; Maze-Unique runs zero-temperature hard moves.
2. Training shapes the landscape WITHOUT differentiating through descent: PL (Eq. 5) = E(x) + per-cell cross-entropy of the EXACT conditional — the partition function cancels per cell, so no inference during training — plus L1 soft-threshold → sparsity (~7.7k nonzero of 263,169 params). Learned weights are readable as the puzzle's rules (Sudoku: correct constraints, zero elsewhere; Maze: dead-end filling).
3. Fitted stop: first board with E ≤ τ + 10⁻³ (τ = highest training-solution energy), else local minimum. CRAM wrong-halts 0%; HRM/TRM/EqR/TRMP/EBR halting rules wrong-halt 0.05–3.54%; EBR's gradient-norm stop fails on Maze entirely. Compute follows difficulty: median FLOPs grow 22× easy→hard (EBR flat 3.3–4.2×10¹¹ in every bin; FRM static).
4. Data efficiency: 30 unique puzzles → 98% (EBR needs 700).
5. Maze-Hard extension: one hidden layer with a strictly convex energy settling to a distance-like field, then answer descent reading it (29 params, two relaxations, 99.9%). Learning hidden quantities WITHOUT task knowledge = their named open problem.
6. Not a general reasoner: ARC-class tasks stay out of reach; the benchmarks are cautions, not wins (their own conclusion).
