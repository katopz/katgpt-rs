## TL;DR

The paper couples looped (weight-tied) depth with an asymmetric Causal Encoder–Decoder (CED — sparse encoder global-KV layers + one projected decoder KV, the DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash topology) and lands three laws: **(1) placement** — recurrence must sit where the global representation is *constructed* (the encoder loop refines the global-KV source across rounds); a decoder loop that re-reads a frozen projection captures only 16–40% of the gain, and a YOCO-style loop over SWA-only layers forfeits ~30% of the long-range gain; **(2) caching** — training under *stochastic recurrence depth* (Poisson mean 8, clip ≥1, backprop through the final 4 of 8 rounds) induces a **contractive fixed point** (per-round state change ρ ≈ 0.61×/round) that makes **last-round KV caching lossless** at inference (≤0.0002 BPB at every scale), while fixed-depth + truncated-BPTT training forms a **period-4 limit cycle** and fails catastrophically (+0.0317 BPB under last-round KV); **(3) serving** — cross-layer (CED) × cross-round (last-round) KV sharing **compound multiplicatively** (36.8× per-token KV cut at 552B, 3.1×–16.5× throughput, agent TTFT −29.7×). It also ships the loop-specific long-context evaluation protocol the field lacked: **offset-averaged position-wise BPB** on long documents plus a **verbatim copy test** — and demonstrates that short-context aggregate BPB *hides* long-range loop failure (the YOCO-U inversion: 97% of average gain, 30% forfeited at range).

**Distilled for katgpt-rs (modelless, inference-time):** the workspace's loop-lane family already ships the serving half structurally — `CacheStrategy::Last` is the `#[default]` in `katgpt-types/src/looping.rs`, `forward_looped` overwrites the same cache slot per position (last-write-wins), and Plan 324 adopted "last-step reuse" with a *measured small loss* (GSM8K 78.92→78.85). This paper supplies the two things that adoption lacked: the **training condition** that makes last-round KV *lossless* rather than approximately harmless (Track B), and an inference-side **contraction certificate** — a measured ρ̂ that certifies cache eligibility per checkpoint *regardless of training provenance*, plus **period-P detection** that converts `CacheStrategy::Mean` from a naive average into a bias-cancelling period-average for cyclic checkpoints. Both land in katgpt-rs Issue 1241 as a typed axis **orthogonal to the halt decision** — the halter answers "when do we stop", the cache verdict answers "what may we cache", and the paper's Appendix D shows they are independent (a contractive-early-halt core is cache-*safe*; a fixed-depth full-depth core is cache-*unsafe*).

## 1. Paper Core Findings

1. **Placement law (where to loop).** On a 12-layer CED (6-layer encoder, L2 = the sole encoder global-KV source, boundary projection at L6), an encoder loop (core L2–5, T=8) captures 89/86/93% of the looped-dense gain at 90M/150M/340M; a decoder loop (identical params, depth, and decode compute) captures only 16/27/40%. The mechanism: the encoder loop **iteratively refines the global-KV source**; the decoder loop repeatedly queries a global KV frozen before the loop began. The gap persists at 5× training (38% at 29.6B tokens) and widens with context.
2. **The YOCO-U inversion (measurement lesson).** An SWA-only looped encoder (all global KV deferred to L6) recovers **97% of the aggregate-BPB gain** yet forfeits **30% of the long-range gain** by end-of-context, with the deficit widening per context doubling on PG19/FinePDFs/GovReport. Short-context evaluation *fabricates* loop quality; position-wise evaluation on long documents (offset-averaged over 6 shifts per sample) exposes it. The verbatim copy test (repeat tokens 0–1023 at 1024–2047; every copied token retrievable only through global KV) isolates the mechanism: the encoder loop removes unlooped CED's recall deficit (−0.160/−0.084 BPB); the decoder loop barely moves it; YOCO-U is worst (+0.105/+0.108 vs dense loop).
3. **Fixed-point training → lossless cross-round KV.** Random recurrence depth (Poisson(8) clipped ≥1, BPTT through the final 4 rounds) drives the core to a **contractive fixed point**: ρ_r shrinks ~0.61×/round (13% → 1.7% at r=8, 0.014% at r=32); layer-2's global KV changes only 3% between rounds 7–8; BPB is flat within 0.0003 from r=8 to r=32 (over-run safe) and ≤0.0004 at 12/16 rounds. Last-round-vs-per-round KV deltas: ≤0.0002 BPB at every scale/budget, both loop placements, dense and CED. The **fixed-depth control fails**: +0.0317 BPB under last-round KV, per-round stability degraded, and Appendix D traces it to a **period-4 limit cycle** (BPB matches its 8-round value only when r ≡ 0 mod 4; ρ never below 30%; layer-2 KV changes 31%/round) — the paper conjectures the period equals the truncated-BPTT window (4).
4. **KV compounding.** CED's cross-layer sharing (×11.6 per-token vs per-layer) × last-round cross-round sharing (×5.2 at T=8) = **×36.8** at DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash scale: one 128K request that OOMs with per-layer per-round KV fits 38× concurrent; agent-trace TTFT −29.7× at c=512; the 2×2 ablation shows the factors multiply exactly (1.27×2.43=3.08, 1.82×2.00=3.64). The cost axis: an encoder loop pays a **prefill premium** (all T rounds on the prefill path; 34 vs 6 layer-passes/prompt-token in their 12L config) while decode cost is placement-invariant; **asymmetric rounds (Tp<Td)** — prefill at Tp=4, decode at Td=8 — retain >99% of the 8-round gain and cut the prefill multiplier 5.1×→2.7×.
5. **Serving honesty.** Capacity numbers run on dummy/original weights *not trained to loop* — lossless last-round KV at 552B is *assumed* from the ≤340M evidence. Per-round KV is emulated by capping the KV pool (identical compute). Quality claims are ≤340M at 2048 context.

**Signal-diffs on every "covered" dismissal (§3.6):**

- `gain_cost_halt` + QRR vs the paper's certificates: the halter's signals (step size, cos θ, concavity floor) gate **stopping**; nothing in its output answers **cache eligibility**, and its Issue-698-T4 record shows `NonContraction` is *refuted as a halt signal* on the InterLoopNorm step-size axis (step size grows to its fixed point there — directional convergence, not contraction). The paper's datum cuts the other way: under contraction *the cache question is separable from the stop question* (early halt is cache-safe; full depth is cache-unsafe on cyclic cores). That separation is the delta; neither the halter nor QRR carries it.
- `loop_depth_probe::write_fractions` vs ρ̂: same *shape* (relative state change), three deltas — (a) it is the residual vs the *state*, not the **ratio of consecutive step norms** (the contraction-decay test needs Δ_r/Δ_{r−1}); (b) report-only probe fuel, no verdict type, no bound; (c) the Issue-717-T6 relative-residual ban applies to *halting*, and this note's usage stays report-only on norm-pinned loop states (RMSNorm between loops) — the exact boundary condition Research 035's redirect wrote into `gain_cost_halt`'s docs. The paper is that law's confirmation instance: ρ_r works precisely because looped-transformer states are norm-pinned.
- `CacheStrategy::{First,Last,Mean}` vs the paper: `Last` ships as default (verified `#[default]`), adopted as an inference heuristic; the paper supplies (i) the *condition* under which it is lossless (training-side, Track B), (ii) the proof it is *catastrophic* without the condition (+0.0317), and (iii) the *rescue* for the failure mode (`Mean` over one detected period). None of the three ship.
