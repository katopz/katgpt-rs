## TL;DR

The second-generation WSP paper asks "should weight-space programming keep the Transformer?" and answers with an architecture-choice experiment: three frontier writers (Claude Opus 5.5, GPT-6.1 Sol, GPT-6 Astra), given ONLY ten labeled ARC questions + an execution sandbox, **free to design** — ALL THREE converge on the same non-transformer: plain-text fact lines compiled by a build script into a **sparse concept table read by a fixed lookup procedure** (no attention, no MLPs, no dense embeddings), up to **54.4%** ARC test accuracy (chance 25%), beating the most accurate released Transformer the same writer wrote with **~1,800× fewer parameters**; their own associative-memory model is ~**680× smaller** than the strongest released arch and up to **17× faster** on GPU than a same-sized Transformer. Two instruments fall out that our corpus lacks:

1. **The interference/traceability census** — every prediction traces to named rows; deleting one written line changes **0.5–1.8** answers (sparse tables) vs **4.8+** (written two-layer Transformers); keeping only the single best row per option ≈ full accuracy. Most written two-layer Transformers — all six of Opus's — score **below plain BM25 over the very text the writer wrote for them** (the paper reads this as dense-key interference); table models mostly score above it. The paper's own framing: "the sparse table pairs few-token keys with hard top-row selection, whereas the Transformer relies on dense, overlapping keys and soft attention" (citing the associative-memory line, Zhong et al. 2025; Wang 2026).
2. **The fixed-K computed-address reader** — a build script folds written lines into a concept table + a **pair table read at computed addresses** from pairs of question concepts; each question reads **120 pair-table rows + one concept-table product**, so GPU time at a fixed concept count is **independent of pair-table size**. This stays ≤3pp under the LLM-designed free-scanning models while flipping the speed sign: across 46 LLM-designed variants timed on an RTX 3090 only ONE runs faster than a same-sized two-layer Transformer (median **9.4× slower**) — the fixed-address read is what turns 9.4×-slower into 17×-faster.

Everything else corroborates the internal lineage: writer convergence on corpus-is-the-model (three labs independently rediscover reflex's engine shape under a no-training constraint); pretrained-checkpoint writers change **<1%** of weights (readout calibration / science-word embeddings — the 980/671 head-surface guardrail posture, at three-writer scale); limits stated honestly (written models recall only what the writer states — CIFAR-10 raw pixels 21–28% vs GD; full hand-overwrites only ever on near-chance checkpoints, never above 72%; up to 50.1% on SciQ while staying near chance on MMLU).

**Distilled for katgpt-rs (modelless, inference-time):** no new mechanism — every load-bearing component maps onto shipped substrate (§1 table). The corpus gains two cheap REPORT-ONLY instruments (BM25-over-author-text floor; leave-one-out row-flip census with the paper's cross-architecture reference bands) filed as reflex Issue 121, and one measured law (fixed-K computed-address reads flip 9.4×-slower → 17×-faster) that is PKM's reason to exist, re-proven at ARC scale.
