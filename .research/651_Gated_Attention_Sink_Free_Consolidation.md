## TL;DR

The paper ablates gating across softmax attention at pretraining scale (30 variants; 15B MoE + 1.7B dense; 3.5T tokens) and lands on one winner: a **head-specific multiplicative sigmoid gate after SDPA** — `Y' = Y ⊙ σ(X·W_θ)` (position G1). Two factors explain it: (1) non-linearity between the merged low-rank `W_v·W_o` map (Eq 6: both projections collapse to one rank-d_k map; GQA sharing worsens it), and (2) **query-dependent sparse gating** (mean score 0.116, distribution concentrated near 0). Payoffs: PPL −0.2, +2 MMLU, loss spikes ~eliminated, 2× max-LR tolerance, attention-sink elimination (first-token share 46.7% → 4.8%), massive activations 11× down (M-Act 1053 → 94), and +10..27 RULER under YaRN 64k–128k context extension.

For this stack the note contributes four things: **§3.1 the Floor Law** — a multiplicative sigmoid gate earns its keep through query-dependent sparsity (gates reaching ~0), so never floor/clamp a gate above 0; additive logit priors (Plan 364's `logit_bias`) stay lawful, multiplicative floors do not. **§3.2 retroactive validation** — the SERVED flagship already implements the paper's best config verbatim: Bonsai-2 is a qwen35-family GDN-hybrid whose full-attention layers ship gated attention (riir-infer Issue 594: interleaved `[q, gate]` from `attn_wq`, `attn_out *= fast_sigmoid(gate)` after SDPA — matching `qwen35.cpp::build_layer_attn` / HF `Qwen3NextAttention`), with the GDN layers' z-gate the same shape on the linear-attention side; keep both as-is. **§3.3 the audit→policy fusion** — the paper's F-Attn/M-Act aggregates as a ONE-TIME offline census selecting `kv_sink_window` posture (→ Issue 981 — runnable NOW on the served flagship, whose gated full-attention layers make it the leading SinkFree candidate; distinct from Plan 287's per-call classifier on both cadence and consumer). **§3.4** the 2026 landscape splits three ways and our school (258 NOP/Broadcast + 566 sinks-as-OT-defaults) already sits on the "sinks are functional" side — the paper's elimination is a TRAINING-time substitution (gates absorb what sinks absorbed; Fig 4: gated hidden states resemble baseline), never an inference-time deletion.

## 1 The paper (compact)

- **Gate family** (Eq 5): `Y' = g(Y, X, W_θ, σ) = Y ⊙ σ(X·W_θ)`; positions G1 (post-SDPA) / G2 (post-V) / G3 (post-K) / G4 (post-Q) / G5 (post-W_O); granularity elementwise vs headwise; head-specific vs shared; multiplicative vs additive; sigmoid vs SiLU.
- **The five ordering laws** (15A2B MoE @400B tokens, Table 1/3/4): G1 > G2 > G3/G4 ≈ baseline > G5 (useless — post-`W_O` non-linearity is a dead end; 6.017 PPL vs baseline 6.026); elementwise 5.761 > headwise 5.792 (ΔPPL 0.031 for 125× fewer params); sigmoid 5.761 > SiLU 5.822; multiplicative 5.761 > additive+SiLU 5.821; head-specific 5.761 > head-shared 5.801; query-dependent (G1, from `X_i`) > value-time (G2, from past `X_j`) 5.820.
- **Stability/scale** (dense 1.7B, Table 2): loss spikes nearly eliminated at 3.5T tokens; baseline diverges at LR 8e-3 (48 layers) while gated converges and improves (7.325 vs diverged); gated@8e-3@bsz4096 is the paper's best dense cell (PPL 7.078); wall-time <2%; headwise gate adds ~1.6M params @15B.
- **Sink elimination** (Table 4): F-Attn (token-0 attention share) 0.467 baseline → 0.048 gated; NS-sigmoid floor variant returns it to 0.451; input-independent gate 0.364; value-gate (G2) kills MAs (1053→125) but sinks persist (0.297) — **MA ⊬ sink**, massive activations are not a sink prerequisite.
- **YaRN interplay** (Table 5): within training length (32k) gated ≈ baseline (+1.7); under YaRN extension the gated model degrades far less — 128k RULER 58.82 vs baseline 31.65 (+27.2); hypothesis: ungated models rely on the sink to redistribute attention and the sink cannot adapt training-free when RoPE changes, while gated models route via input-dependent gates.
- **Params-free relatives** (Table 3): per-head RMSNorm at G1 (zero params) recovers much of the PPL gain (5.847 vs gate 5.761 vs baseline 6.026); SiLU-only at G1 modest (5.975). All co-trained — retrofit efficacy on untrained checkpoints is NOT established by this paper.
- **Switch-Head disentangle** (A.1): "Switch v, 1top1" (= a plain value-headwise gate) matches the full expert-routing variants — the gate itself, not the routing, carries the gains. Relevant to any MoE-attention program (cf. Plan 279's router).

## 3 What this note adds

### 3.1 The Floor Law (NS-sigmoid × 566)

**Law: a multiplicative sigmoid gate earns its keep through query-dependent SPARSITY — gates must be able to reach ~0. Never floor or clamp a gate above 0.**

Evidence (Table 4 row 7): NS-sigmoid `0.5 + 0.5·σ(x)` (range [0.5, 1.0]) keeps the non-linearity but removes the sparsity: PPL 5.900 vs 5.761, **the attention sink returns** (F-Attn 0.451 vs 0.048), M-Act balloons back (892 vs 94). Sparsity, not non-linearity, is the operative ingredient for the sink-free posture; non-linearity alone buys only part of the PPL gain (RMSNorm-G1 5.847, SiLU-only 5.975).

The 566 lens explains the shape: in `g*_j = σ(s_j/τ + logit π_j)`, a hard floor at 0.5 bounds the gate's logit below by 0 — the gate can no longer express "suppress this key", which is exactly the query-dependent filtering the paper credits (Fig 4: gated hidden states resemble baseline; gating serves the absorption function the sink served). Two consequences, both checked against shipped code:

- **Lawful:** constant ADDITIVE priors (`logit π_j`, Plan 364's `logit_bias`) — they shift logits but preserve the full [0, 1) range; suppression stays expressible. 566's margin law (δ ~ ln L) governs how much prior a low-signal regime may carry; the paper adds the pretraining-scale evidence that the dynamic range itself must survive.
- **Unlawful:** multiplicative floors/clamps (`max(g, 0.1)`, NS-sigmoid, any `0.5 + 0.5σ` spelling). Grep evidence of no shipped violation: no `0.5 + 0.5`/shifted-sigmoid gate patterns in `katgpt-rs/crates` or `riir-infer/src|crates`; no `.clamp(0.x)` floors in `katgpt-core/src/hla`; BOTH shipped gate sites use plain `fast_sigmoid` — the full-attention `gate_buf` (Issue 594) and the GDN `attn_gate` (both verified floor-free in verdict round 1). This is a design law for every FUTURE gate (EGA arms, sigmoid-attention arms, any trained gate) — recorded here because the paper is the first pretraining-scale falsification of the floored variant.

### 3.2 Retroactive validation — the flagship's full-attention layers ARE this paper (riir-infer Issue 594)

`riir-infer/src/deltanet/forward.rs` `forward_attention_layer` (~lines 728–857) implements **Qwen3.5 gated attention** for the hybrid's `full_attention` layers: `attn_wq` produces `2*q_dim` outputs laid out per-head interleaved as `[q(hd), gate(hd)]`, and after SDPA — before `o_proj` — `attn_out[i] *= fast_sigmoid(gate_buf[i])` (the code comment cites `qwen35.cpp::build_layer_attn` and HuggingFace `Qwen3NextAttention.forward`). That is the paper's winning configuration VERBATIM: post-SDPA, query-time (the gate rides the current token's input projection), elementwise, multiplicative, sigmoid — G1-elementwise-query-dependent. **Bonsai-2-27B, the served ternary flagship, is a qwen35-family GDN-hybrid whose full-attention layers are gated attention; no change warranted anywhere.**

The second site, `attn_out *= fast_sigmoid(attn_gate)` at forward.rs:2507/2832, is the **GDN (linear) layers' z-gate** (`attn_gate` → `in_proj_z` per the loader; `rotation.rs:234,269` classifies it a DeltaNet-layer tensor) — the same gating family on the linear-attention side, with no SDPA; an instance of what the paper's §1 catalogues (Gated DeltaNet), not a softmax-attention G1. (Round-1 correction: this note originally cited the z-gate site as the validation; the reviewer's grep found the full-attention site.)

The paper also validates the house doctrine empirically at pretraining scale: **sigmoid > SiLU** for multiplicative gates (5.761 vs 5.822), **multiplicative > additive** (5.761 vs 5.821), and — via the A.1 Switch-Head disentangle — plain sigmoid gating over expert-routing complexity. Both shipped gates use plain `fast_sigmoid` (range (0,1), no floor), consistent with §3.1's law.

### 3.3 Audit→KV-policy fusion (→ Issue 981)

The paper's two census aggregates — **F-Attn** (layer-mean token-0 attention share; calibrated classes 0.048–0.073 sink-free vs 0.297–0.467 sunk) and **M-Act** (mean per-layer max activation; 94 gated vs 1053 baseline) — exist as primitives here in pieces: `sink_classify::SinkDiagnostic.strength` already measures attention mass on sink positions per head; `fitted_v_gate::sink_norms_report` reports the sink-window norm half. What is missing is the SERVING-side consumer: `kv_sink_window` pins the first N positions unconditionally (`n_sink` default 4) in every eviction lane, and `q8kv_sink_guard` carries f32 `sink_rows` for them — with no measurement deciding whether the model sinks at all.

Issue 981 wires the pieces into a ONE-TIME offline audit (boot-time or CLI, per checkpoint — never per-call: Plan 287's G3 latency FAIL is the recorded reason that path died) that selects the `SinkWindowPolicy` posture: SinkFree ⇒ pin from the per-position profile with a non-zero floor (≥ 1 until T4 proves 0 safe) + shrink the q8kv sink sidecar; SinkHeavy ⇒ incumbent pin + a long-context-risk line (the Table-5 YaRN result turned into a pre-serve flag); Undecided (the 0.10–0.25 chosen band) ⇒ incumbent posture, disclosed.

**Signal-diff vs Plan 287 (sink_aware_attn):** 287 classifies NOP-vs-Broadcast sinks per call INSIDE the engine's sigmoid attention and gates NOP sinks in the forward — a different consumer (the forward, not KV eviction) and a different cadence (per call, which is exactly what failed G3). 981 is once-per-checkpoint, offline, and acts on the eviction policy the serving lanes already run. 287 stays opt-in-diagnostic; 981 composes with it (the audit can feed 287's classifier as a prior), it does not replace it.

**Honest weakest point, named (revised after verdict round 1):** the census must isolate the FULL-ATTENTION layers — the GDN half has no softmax sink to measure, so a blended F-Attn dilutes the signal; and the paper's F-Attn is token-0-only, so T1 must compute a per-position mass profile over the first N positions (secondary/delimiter sinks at positions 1–3 would otherwise hide behind a low token-0 share). With those handled, the relax-pinning half is runnable NOW on the served flagship: Bonsai's full-attention layers are gated (§3.2), making it the leading SinkFree candidate — the audit decides, not the convention. (Round-1 framing error — "may never fire on today's fleet" — is corrected here and in Issue 981.)

### 3.4 Landscape (searched 2026-11-05; 6 queries + follow-ups)

- **Not superseded** — NeurIPS 2025 Best Paper, ~402 citations, productionized in Qwen3-Next (3:1 GDN : gated-attention hybrid; GGUF/llama.cpp support parses the gate tensors).
- **2026 counterpoint school** (three works): sinks forge implicit MoE structure in attention (arXiv:2602.01203), massive activations act as gradient regulators (arXiv:2603.17771), and a "provably necessary" counterclaim for trigger-conditional tasks; plus the survey arXiv:2604.10098 naming the families. **Our stack already sits on this side**: 258's NOP/Broadcast duality and 566's sinks-as-optimal-transport-defaults say sinks are FUNCTIONAL at inference; the paper's own Fig 4 agrees at training time — gates SUBSTITUTED the sink's absorption function rather than deleting it. Elimination is a training-time architectural substitution; at inference time on ungated checkpoints the answer remains sink-aware management (pinning, 287-style classification), never deletion.
- **Family disambiguation:** the Forgetting Transformer (arXiv:2503.02130, FoX) gates the unnormalized LOGITS pre-softmax (data-dependent forget factor, additive in log-space) — a different family from gate-on-output; do not merge the two in future greps (the paper itself cites FoX as parallel, not prior).
- **Name lineage (inference, flagged):** the internal arch naming is qwen35-family (`qwen35-hybrid`, forward.rs:2938; `Qwen3NextAttention` in the Issue-594 comment) — our flagship IS a Qwen3-Next-lineage architecture, i.e. the productionization of this very paper. The external "Qwen3.8-Flash-Next" (Aug 2026) reportedly reworks the pairing toward GDN + Qwen Sparse Attention (QSA) — a LATER generation than our ternary GGUF checkpoints; if the league ever tracks that generation, QSA (not vanilla gated attention) is the next serve-target shape.
